What a perfusionist
actually runs.

Follow the blood — blue leaves the patient, turns red in the oxygenator and returns. Every part of this loop is the perfusionist’s responsibility.

Overview

The heart‑lung machine

During cardiac surgery the heart is stopped. Cardiopulmonary bypass takes over: blood drains from the patient, is oxygenated outside the body and pumped back — while the perfusionist controls every flow, pressure and gas. Hover any component.

Patient

Cannulae & the patient

A venous cannula drains blood from the right atrium or venae cavae; an aortic cannula returns oxygenated blood to the systemic circulation.

01 · Reservoir

Venous reservoir

Collects venous return by gravity — or with vacuum‑assisted venous drainage (VAVD), which Mosa managed during a pediatric heart transplant.

02 · Arterial pump

Arterial pump

A roller or centrifugal pump generates systemic flow, calculated from body surface area and continuously adjusted to pressures and perfusion.

03 · Oxygenator

Membrane oxygenator

Adds oxygen, removes carbon dioxide and — through its heat exchanger — controls patient temperature. Monitored with serial blood gas analysis.

04 · Arterial filter

Arterial line filter

Traps micro‑emboli and air before blood returns to the patient through the arterial line.

05 · Cardioplegia

Cardioplegia delivery

Delivers a cold, high‑potassium solution to the coronary arteries to arrest the heart and protect the myocardium during the operation.

06 · Gas blender

Gas blender & sweep

Mixes air and oxygen into the sweep gas that sets oxygenation and CO₂ removal. Troubleshooting blender and sweep‑gas failures is core emergency training.
